PrimeBank Banking Analytics & Fraud Analysis (SQL)

Overview

This project presents an end-to-end SQL analysis of a synthetic retail banking dataset for PrimeBank, covering approximately 60,000 customers, 202,831 card transactions, 22,000 loans, customer accounts, payment cards, and operations across 136 branches. The analysis transforms raw banking data into actionable business insights, focusing on customer behaviour, credit risk, fraud detection, operational efficiency, and branch performance.

Using SQL, the project explores customer demographics, transaction patterns, loan portfolio health, payment card management, fraud trends, and data quality to support data-driven decision-making within a retail banking environment.

⸻

Project Objectives

This project aims to:
	•	Analyse customer demographics and acquisition trends.
	•	Evaluate account activity and identify dormant accounts.
	•	Assess transaction behaviour and customer cash flow.
	•	Investigate payment card utilisation and lifecycle management.
	•	Measure loan portfolio performance across different loan statuses.
	•	Identify high-risk loan products and branch-level credit risk.
	•	Analyse fraudulent transactions and identify fraud trends.
	•	Detect data quality issues affecting business reporting.
	•	Generate strategic recommendations to improve operational efficiency, fraud prevention, and credit risk management.

⸻

Key Insights
	•	Analysed approximately 60,000 customers across 136 branches.
	•	Evaluated a loan portfolio containing approximately 22,000 loans.
	•	Analysed 202,831 card transactions and identified 1,037 fraudulent transactions, representing a 0.51% fraud rate.
	•	Recorded more than £1.79 million in total fraud losses across the analysed period.
	•	2023 experienced the highest fraud activity, with 150 fraud cases and £246,652.03 in financial losses.
	•	January recorded the highest monthly fraud volume (103 cases), while November recorded the fewest (66 cases).
	•	Found that no customer experienced multiple reported fraud incidents, indicating fraud was dispersed across the customer base rather than concentrated among repeat victims.
	•	Identified 3,530 dormant accounts holding approximately ₹4.72 billion (£36.74 million) in customer funds.
	•	Discovered 40,945 expired payment cards, highlighting opportunities to improve card lifecycle management.
	•	Determined that 90.25% of total loan value was either Active or Closed, while 9.75% was classified as Defaulted or Written Off.
	•	Identified Education Loans and Gold Loans as the highest-risk lending products.
	•	Revealed significant differences in default rates between branches, highlighting opportunities to strengthen underwriting standards and portfolio management.
	•	Identified data quality issues involving closed loans with missing repayment records.

Business Value

This project demonstrates how SQL can be applied to solve real-world banking challenges by converting transactional and operational data into meaningful business intelligence. The analysis provides insights into customer behaviour, lending performance, fraud exposure, branch operations, and data quality. The findings support stronger credit risk management, improved fraud detection, enhanced operational efficiency, and better strategic decision-making, enabling financial institutions to reduce risk, optimise performance, and improve long-term profitability.
